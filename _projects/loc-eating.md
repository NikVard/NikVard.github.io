## The question

Loss-of-control eating, the feeling of not being able to stop, can be very hard to treat. A clinical trial in our lab is testing responsive deep brain stimulation, a device that switches on only when it detects a specific brain signal. To find that signal, we need to know how a reward area deep in the brain, near the nucleus accumbens, responds to food.

## What happens in the operating room

Patients stay awake during part of their implant surgery. While a very fine electrode records from the target area, they take part in short tasks:

- looking at pictures of tempting foods, plain foods, and everyday objects;
- tasting a milkshake (sweet and fatty) and, for comparison, a tasteless liquid.

## My role

I run the recordings in the operating room and analyse the data afterwards. The setting is fast-paced and every minute counts, so the recordings have to be right the first time. We also talk with the patients throughout, since they are awake and doing the tasks with us during brain surgery.

## Why it matters to me

The science is fascinating, but mostly I am glad to be part of these patients' journey.
