## The question

Can we detect periods of distress in people with OCD, close to real time, using only one kind of signal - fast "gamma" rhythms from the front of the brain?

If so, that signal could one day tell a brain stimulator when to step in, and it can be measured outside the hospital, during everyday life.

## What I do

The patients in this study have two separate implanted pulse generators, also called neurostimulators: Medtronic Percept™ implanted pulse generators (IPGs), medical devices that deliver deep brain stimulation, and can also record brain activity. I collect recordings from both devices at the same time, together with EEG from the scalp, while patients go through periods of controlled symptom provocations.

The tricky part is that three separate recording systems each keep their own clock. Using small, harmless electrical pulses delivered on the skin to leave a mark that all the devices pick up, I can align these independent signals (including the patient's behaviour) on a single timeline. This follows the method described by Michaela Alarie and colleagues ([Alarie et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2023.09.005)), which we adopted in the lab. Many thanks to Michaela, a colleague in our lab since July 2026, for her work and help.

## Highlights

- A practical method for synchronising two implanted stimulators with scalp EEG and behaviour, which is usually one of the hardest parts of this kind of study.
- The project is still collecting data. Results will follow once the study is complete.
